Do the tiers mean anything?

Average score of the players in each tier as shown when you set waivers (the Tuesday sync; rebuilt weeks: before their first game), all weeks together, with the number of player-games. Higher tiers should score more.

The ± is the margin of error (about 95%): one game swings a player's score by around 7 points, while the waiver players a position shows are usually projected within 2 to 4 points of each other. Where two tiers' ranges overlap, the games so far can't tell them apart yet, even if the tiers are right.

Who gets how much say

Projections (Sleeper and ESPN) get 60% of a player's number and rankings (FantasyPros) 40%, split evenly within each: Sleeper 30%, ESPN 30%, FantasyPros 40% when every source has him. A ranking becomes points by giving rank N the Nth-best projection among the players it ranks. Where a source has nothing for a player, the others' shares grow to fill its place. Once enough weeks are scored, each source's share will follow its accuracy.

What these mean
  • Avg miss: the average gap between our number and what the player scored. Lower is better.
  • Simple guess: the player's own average so far this season (players with 2+ games). If we don't beat it, the projections add nothing.
  • Runs high/low: whether our numbers are too high or too low on average.
  • Ranks players right: how well the order of our numbers matches the order of the scores (0 = no better than random, 1 = perfect). Shown only once there are 100+ player-games.
  • Inside ±1 / ±2 range: how often the score landed within one or two "typical swings" of our number. About 68% and 95% means the ranges are honest.
  • Typical miss (RMSE): like the average miss, but big misses count extra.
  • Top-12 hits: of our 12 best-projected, how many finished in the actual top 12 at the position (ties at 12th count). Picking 12 of the 50 at random would land about 3.
  • Correlation: how closely our numbers and the scores rise and fall together (0 = not at all, 1 = perfectly).
  • Odds score (Brier): how good our win probabilities are. A coin flip scores 0.250; lower is better.
  • R²: below 0 means a flat guess of the average would have done better.
